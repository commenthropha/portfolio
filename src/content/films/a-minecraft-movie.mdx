Writing a serious, unsatirical review of A Minecraft Movie feels impossible; this movie was memed into cultural ubiquity before it was even released. To sum the movie up, it exists as a platform for Jack Black to be himself while calling himself "Steve" (which, to be honest, isn't a bad shout). All jokes aside, and for lack of a better descriptor, this movie is kinda buns. Besides the expectedly enjoyable Jack Black performance, the movie feels like a slightly disjointed experience that isn't entirely sure what it's trying to be. The film operates on a Jumanji-esque dynamic, pulling together a rag-tag group of characters from completely different backgrounds into a Minecraft world where they must work together to make it back to the real world. It's as formulaic as one may expect with little ambition or aspiration to do something novel. You could swap out the Minecraft theme for any equally whimscal world and the movie would not lose any of its substance.

To that extent, the movie's biggest flaw is the lack of how the narrative aims to understand or care about what made the source material worth adapting in the first place. And this seems to be the norm with video game adaptations, which have a long and storied history of prioritising the recognisable iconography over the actual essence of why people fall in love with the video games in the first place; the best adaptations understand that the iconography is only valuable insofar as it serves a story that feels true to what the games actually are.

Sonic the Hedgehog 3 is probably the best video game adaptation that I've seen and it isn't particularly close. The movie fully understands the balance between fanservice, appealing to a wide audience, and telling a compelling narrative. Although I have some reservations about the way that Sonic is characterised in these movies, the writers understand the franchise's characters enough to write them with meaningful arcs and emotions. Moreover, the fanservice in the movie lands because it feels as though it's earned rather than deployed as a mechanism to gain favourable audience reviews. The entire third act of Sonic 3, up to and including the super transformations with Live and Learn, is some of the most fun I've had watching a movie in theaters, with the fight between Super Sonic and Super Shadow feeling reminiscent of a Dragon Ball Z fight as it always should have been.

This makes it all the more confusing that The Super Mario Galaxy Movie made almost **twice** the amount that Sonic the Hedgehog 3 did at the box office. Much like how A Minecraft Movie was Jumanji with a Minecraft skin (no pun intended), The Super Mario Galaxy Movie stands as an Illumination movie with a Mario facade; Lumas, the enchanting, mystical cosmic creatures, were essentially just Mario-ified minions. Ultimately, The Super Mario Galaxy Movie is a prime example of iconography mistaken for substance - a film that is perfectly content to posture with the things that you love from the games without doing anything particularly meaningful with them.

A Minecraft Movie will probably be remembered in the same calibre as the Mario movies - loud, profitable, but ultimately hollow. Which, come to think of it, is a fairly apt descriptor of a cave in Minecraft before you've had a chance to mine it for ores.
