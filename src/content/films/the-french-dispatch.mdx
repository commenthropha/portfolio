I really wanted to like The French Dispatch. But somewhere in the middle of the seventeenth symmetrical shot of someone standing in a pastel-coloured room, it slowly dawned on me that this movie is the definition of style over substance.

The film is an anthology of sorts, structured around the final edition of an American magazine published out of a fictional French town. It is intended to be a love letter to journalism as well as mid-century French culture. Each segment is crafted with the same meticulous beauty that defines all of his recent work, which is to say it looks absolutely stunning and, yet, feels strangely hollow.

Owen Wilson shows up early on as a cycling journalist narrating a tour of the town, and he’s an immediate breath of fresh air; his delivery is effortlessly charming in a film that otherwise feels like it has been pressed and laminated. But maybe thats because it’s Owen Wilson, in the same way that if this scene had Willem DeFoe I’d be equally captivated. However, the film moves on quickly and returns to its default register of arch, deadpan whimsy, and his segment ends up feeling like a glimpse of a more enjoyable film that never quite arrives.

This is the central problem with The French Dispatch, and arguably with Anderson’s recent filmography more broadly. There was a point with Fantastic Mr Fox and The Grand Budapest Hotel where the stylistic idiosyncrasies felt like they were in service of the narrative. Now it increasingly feels like Wes Anderson’s distinctive style is the point, mistaking filmmaking intricacy for depth. Style over substance isn’t a new criticism of Wes Anderson, but The French Dispatch is perhaps the clearest case that I’ve seen that the balance has tipped too far.

<Quote>
  The exhibition, Wes Anderson: The Archives, charts the evolution of the

  Featuring over 600 objects from Anderson’s personal collection, explore how
  his distinctive vision and attention to detail have created some of the most

And I believe that, in a sense, the exhibition is an apt descriptor of the way I felt watching this movie. By the end I felt less like I’d watched a film and more like I’d been walked through a very expensive exhibition by a curator who was far more interested in the curation than in whether I was actually moved by any of it.
